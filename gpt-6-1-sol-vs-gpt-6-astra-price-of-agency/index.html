GPT-6.1 Sol vs GPT-6 Astra: The Price of Agency

"A fifth of the price."

That was the headline.

It's true.

It just isn't the price you think it is.

GPT-6.1 Sol costs one-fifth of GPT-6 Astra's standard token prices. [Stated, OpenAI]

It isn't five times cheaper than the Sol it replaced.

On input and output, Sol and GPT-6.1 Sol share the same list price. [Stated, platform.openai.com/docs/pricing, 13 Oct 2026]

Only cached input got cheaper: US$0.20 to US$0.10 at the standard context tier. [Stated]

The cut was never per token.

It was per decision about which model to call.

That's the price of agency.

01, Read the table, not the headline

OpenAI prices two ways: up to 272K tokens of context, and above 272K. [Stated, platform.openai.com/docs/pricing, 13 Oct 2026]

Per million tokens, standard (non-batch) tier:

Model Context Input Output Cached input
GPT-6 Astra ≤272K US$10 US$50 US$1.00
GPT-6 Astra >272K US$20 US$75 US$2.00
GPT-6 Sol ≤272K US$2 US$10 US$0.20
GPT-6 Sol >272K US$4 US$15 US$0.40
GPT-6.1 Sol ≤272K US$2 US$10 US$0.10
GPT-6.1 Sol >272K US$4 US$15 US$0.20

Sol to Sol: input unchanged.

Output unchanged.

Cached input halved.

The 5× gap is Sol versus Astra, at the standard tier.

OpenAI's pitch is that Sol now gets "near-Astra" results on its own benchmarks. [Stated, OpenAI]

So the real announcement is a quality claim, not a price cut.

The model got better.

The meter stayed where it was.

02, Who captures the margin?

Model costs fall.

Prices don't have to follow.

Gartner says it directly.

By 2030, running inference on a trillion-parameter model will cost providers over 90% less than in 2025. [Estimate, Gartner, 25 Mar 2026]

In the same release: those falling provider costs "will not be fully passed on to enterprise customers." [Stated, Gartner, 25 Mar 2026]

Read those two lines together.

The provider's cost curve and your price curve are different curves.

The gap between them is margin.

When a better model ships at the old price, the gain shows up as quality for you and margin for the provider.

Neither is a scandal.

Just know which one you're buying.

03, Agency multiplies the meter

A flat per-token price is not a flat bill.

Agents don't ask once.

They plan, call tools, check, retry.

Gartner: agentic models need 5–30× the tokens per task of a standard chatbot. [Estimate, Gartner, 25 Mar 2026]

Gartner again, five months later: inference cost per agentic workflow will rise more than fivefold through 2028. [Estimate, Gartner, 17 Aug 2026]

And: routing a task to an agentic reasoning model raises provider inference cost by "at least five times, and often much more." [Stated, Gartner, 17 Aug 2026]

Token prices flat or falling.

Tokens per task rising faster.

That's the inversion The Loop Left the Chat Window described.

Same mechanism.

New price list.

04, The real lever is the router

If Sol is near-Astra at a fifth of the price, the economic question isn't "which model is best?"

It's "which model does this step need?"

Gartner's own advice: route routine, high-frequency tasks to smaller, more efficient models, and keep frontier inference "heavily gated." [Stated, Gartner, 25 Mar 2026]

That's what the 5× gap rewards.

Not loyalty to the flagship.

Discipline about when to call it.

Worked example: the same agent, two routing policies

One agent task: 20 model calls, 4,000 input and 1,000 output tokens per call, no caching.

Prices at the ≤272K tier. [Stated, 13 Oct 2026]

Policy Calls on Astra Calls on Sol Cost per task
Everything on Astra 20 0 US$1.80
Everything on Sol 0 20 US$0.36
Route: hard steps to Astra 4 16 US$0.65

Arithmetic per call: Astra = 4,000 × $10/1M + 1,000 × $50/1M = $0.09. Sol = 4,000 × $2/1M + 1,000 × $10/1M = $0.018.

Same task.

5× spread.

The model didn't change the bill.

The router did.

05, Where flat prices turn into rent

Per-token pricing assumes you decide when to ask.

Always-on agents don't wait to be asked.

In OpenAI Dots and the Codex Daemon, the units changed: dots, speed, monthly capacity.

OpenAI hasn't published what a dot's work costs. [Stated, OpenAI]

Dots run on GPT-6 Astra. [Stated, OpenAI]

So the only public anchor for background work is Astra's list: US$50 per million output at ≤272K, US$75 above. [Stated]

The flagship.

Not the fifth-of-the-price model.

When the agent decides when to think, the vendor decides which model thinks.

That's worth knowing before you sign.

The pattern across all of them:

Is the flagship's price falling?

Or is a cheaper tier getting better, at the old cheaper price?

Those are different stories.

Only one of them is a price cut.

07, The buyer's checklist

Before you believe a price headline:

Compared with what? The model it replaces, or the flagship above it?

Which line moved? Input, output, or cached input?

What's the context tier? OpenAI jumps at 272K. Haiku jumps at 100K.

What's your token shape? Output-heavy agents feel output prices, not input prices.

Who picks the model? You, your router, or the vendor's agent?

What's the unit? Tokens, seats, agents, or monthly capacity?

What's the date? Price lists move. Every number needs one.

OpenAI Dots and the Codex Daemon: Always-On AI Agents Turn Tokens Into Rent

You used to rent a model by the call.

Ask. Pay. Done.

Now the agent moves in.

It has its own computer.

It works while you sleep.

It updates itself.

You don't pay per question any more.

You pay to keep it living there.

That's rent.

01, From landlord to tenant-in-residence

In The Silent Landlord the argument was simple.

Google doesn't just rent you the house.

It owns the land, the power and a stake in the neighbours.

This is the next floor up.

The agent itself is becoming a resident.

Two releases within days of each other show the shape.

OpenAI Dots (29 Sep 2026): "always-on agents" with their own cloud computer, working "24/7." [Stated]

The Codex CLI daemon (reported 25 Sep 2026): a persistent, self-updating background process that starts by default and keeps running after you close every window. [Reported, GitHub issue]

One is a product.

One is a default.

Both point the same way.

The loop never closes.

02, What a dot actually is

OpenAI's own description. [Stated]

  • Powered by GPT-6 Astra.
  • Its own cloud computer and its own browser.
  • Connects to over 4,000 apps through plugins.
  • Reachable in ChatGPT, Slack and Teams, with texting coming.
  • Learns your preferences over time.
  • When you're not working with it, it does "proactive research" in the background, using your connected apps through read-only tools.

The pitch: work done the way you'd do it, "sometimes before you even think to ask."

OpenAI's own example: an early tester's dot noticed he'd forgotten to invoice a publication, prepared the invoice and sent it after his approval. [Stated]

That's genuinely useful.

It's also a resident with keys.

03, How the pricing changes shape

Read the commercial terms slowly. [Stated]

Your first dot is included in Pro or Business Premium at no extra cost.

The plan includes "an allowance for deeper work."

In future you'll be able to add more dots.

And "scale the output of each dot by either increasing its speed or the total amount of work it can take on per month."

Business Premium, one of the plans that includes a dot, is US$100 per seat per month billed annually, or US$125 billed monthly, with a minimum of 2 users. [Stated, OpenAI]

Pro also includes it: Pro 100 at US$100 a month, Pro 200 at US$200, Pro 500 at US$500. [Stated, OpenAI, About ChatGPT Pro tiers]

Monthly billing only.

Ultrafast is only in Pro 500. [Stated]

Conversations with your dot don't count toward usage limits.

Tasks it starts in Codex or ChatGPT Work do.

OpenAI hasn't said what a dot's work costs. [Stated]

The only anchor: dots run on GPT-6 Astra. [Stated, OpenAI]

At the standard context tier (up to 272K tokens), Astra lists at US$10 per million input and US$50 per million output. [Stated, platform.openai.com/docs/pricing, 13 Oct 2026]

Above 272K: US$20 input, US$75 output. [Stated]

At the standard tier, every million output tokens is US$50.

No per-dot figure exists.

Look at the units.

Not tokens.

Dots.

Speed.

Monthly capacity.

That's a lease, with tiers.

Per-token pricing was metered utility.

You saw the meter.

You could stop asking.

An always-on agent never stops asking on your behalf.

So the bill can't sit on each question.

It has to sit on residency: how many agents live here, how fast they run, how much they may do per month.

That's rent.

04, The quieter version: the daemon nobody asked for

The Codex daemon is what rent looks like before it has a price.

A GitHub issue filed against Codex CLI 0.157.0 describes it. [Reported]

Opening an interactive session starts a persistent background daemon.

It keeps running after every Codex window closes, until you stop it or reboot.

It copies the whole platform package, about 374 MB, into its own directory and runs from that copy.

From then on, the backend's version is decoupled from the one your package manager installed.

A detached updater checks five minutes after start, then hourly, downloads new releases and restarts the backend.

The issue reports restarts can kill active turns.

It inherits the environment of whichever session started it, including proxy settings.

The stop command leaves the updater running.

The ask is modest: make it opt-in.

As of 13 Oct 2026 the issue is still open, with no public reply from OpenAI. [Reported]

The opt-out in config.toml works, users confirm. [Reported]

Fair contrast: Claude Code also runs a background supervisor. [Stated, Claude Code docs]

But it starts it when you ask for background work. [Stated]

Codex starts its daemon every interactive session. [Reported]

One is a feature you call.

The other is a default you discover.

At the time of writing, Hacker News had given it 423 points. [Reported]

Dots got 769. [Reported]

People noticed.

05, Why this is a control question, not a convenience one

A per-call model leaves on its own.

A resident agent stays.

Five things change when it stays.

1. Presence becomes the product. The value is that it's always there. So the price follows presence, not use.

2. Updates arrive on the vendor's clock. A self-updating daemon runs the version the vendor ships, not the one you installed. [Reported] Change control just moved out of your hands.

3. Background work is invisible work. Dots offer Activity View to follow background progress. [Stated] That's the right instinct. But you have to look.

4. Identity sprawls. A dot connects to apps, other devices and, with permission, your laptop. [Stated] OpenAI is previewing specialist dots with "their own identity for access management." [Stated] Every resident needs its own lease, its own keys and its own record.

5. Leaving gets harder every month. The more your dot "learns your preferences, how you think," the more of that sits inside the vendor's loop. [Stated] That's the swap test from The Loop Left the Chat Window again. Can you move the agent without losing what it learned?

06, Credit where it's due

OpenAI built real safeguards into Dots. [Stated]

  • Each dot works on its own cloud computer, separate from yours unless you connect it.
  • Proactive background research uses read-only tools: no sending messages, editing content or controlling your browser.
  • Custom Rules let you allow, require approval for, or block actions.
  • Auto-review checks actions against your instructions and rules.
  • Some sensitive tasks, like changing a password, always stay with you.
  • Monitoring can pause or stop a dot.
  • Business, Enterprise and Edu content isn't used for training by default.

That's the right design direction.

The landlord installed good locks.

It's still the landlord's building.

07, The tenant's checklist

Before an always-on agent moves in, get answers.

What's the lease unit? Seats, agents, speed, monthly work, or tokens? Which one grows when usage grows?

Who controls updates? Can you pin a version? Does it update while working?

What runs when you're not looking? Is background activity logged somewhere you'll actually read?

What can it touch, read-only versus write? Is that enforced in the tools, or only in the instructions?

What happens when you leave? Can you export what it learned, or does it stay behind?

Can you evict it in one command? Fully, including the updater?

If the honest answer to the last one is "mostly," that's your answer.

A. Codex CLI background daemon: what the issue documents [Reported, github.com/openai/codex/issues/48195, filed 25 Sep 2026]

Behaviour Detail
Version Codex CLI 0.157.0, npm install, Linux (WSL2)
Trigger Opening any interactive codex session
Process codex app-server --managed-daemon, no TTY, reparented to PID 1
Lifetime Survives all windows closing, until daemon stop or reboot
Disk ~374 MB package copied into ~/.codex/packages/app-server-daemon/releases/<version>/
Updater Detached pid-update-loop: first check at 5 min, then hourly; downloads and restarts the backend
Side effects Restarts can kill active turns; inherits the first session's proxy environment
Bug daemon stop leaves the updater running
Workaround codex features disable daemon_auto_start, then stop the daemon, kill the updater and remove the package directory
Ask Make daemon_auto_start opt-in for the CLI
Status (13 Oct 2026) Open; 15 comments, none from OpenAI staff; only automated labels

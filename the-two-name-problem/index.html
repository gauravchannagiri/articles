01 · The Two-Name Problem

The demand is not the problem

It has become fashionable to call this a bubble.

The disclosures don't support that.

Google Cloud's backlog went from $106B to $514B in a year. Filed

Cloud revenue grew 82%, to $24.768B in the quarter. Filed

Sundar Pichai stated the binding constraint is physical supply, not demand. Stated

Just over half the backlog is scheduled to convert to recognised revenue within 24 months. Stated

That is not speculative pipeline.

That is a delivery schedule.

02 · The Two-Name Problem

Two names, half the forward revenue

A backlog roughly five times annualised revenue is the signature of a few very large multi-year commitments.

Not many mid-sized customers standardising.

UBS estimates OpenAI and Anthropic account for 27% of Google Cloud's 2026 revenue. Estimate

Rising above 48% in 2027. Estimate

Over $124B combined, with Anthropic alone modelled at $76B. Estimate

The weight sits with Anthropic.

OpenAI holds large committed capacity with Azure and Oracle, so its Google share is the smaller and more contestable half.

That's the right place to push on the estimate.

It doesn't move the Anthropic figure.

That's where the concentration lives.

03 · The Two-Name Problem

The earnings beat was a paper gain

Alphabet reported Q2 EPS of $9.11 against a consensus of $2.89. Filed

A clear beat.

Until you read what's inside it.

The figure included roughly $99B in pre-tax gains on equity stakes, including SpaceX and Anthropic. Filed

After tax, about $6.26 a share.

Strip it out and operational EPS was closer to $2.85.

A miss.
Reported EPS $9.11
Pre-tax equity-stake gains ~$99B (≈ $6.26/share after tax)
Operational EPS ~$2.85 vs $2.89 consensus

Marking stakes to market is standard accounting.

That isn't the interesting part.

The reflexivity is.

The headline quarter was carried by the rising value of counterparties that are also among Alphabet's largest customers.

If foundation-lab valuations compress, Alphabet takes the hit twice in one event.

The equity asset.

And the contracted cloud receivable.

Not two exposures.

One bet, booked in two places.

06 · The Two-Name Problem

Same numbers, different conclusion

Ed Zitron has been making the concentration argument loudly since July.

On Bloomberg and in his newsletter, compiling analyst notes from UBS, Wells Fargo and Barclays.

He is right about the numbers.

I read what they mean differently.

The bear case (Zitron, as he argues it):

  • 70%+ of AI revenue at Microsoft, Google and Amazon traces to OpenAI or Anthropic.
  • OpenAI lost $20.9B in 2025. Neither lab pays from operating cash flow.
  • 190GW of planned capacity implies $1.6T+ in annual demand that does not exist.
  • Conclusion: circular financing dressed as growth.

Where I land:

  • Pichai stated the binding constraint is supply, not demand. Fake demand doesn't come with a supply ceiling.
  • Over half the $514B backlog converts within 24 months. A delivery schedule, not a pipeline.
  • Third-party TPU sales and the Blackstone JV are revenue lines outside the two labs.
  • Conclusion: the demand is real. The revenue base is narrow. Different problems.
07 · The Two-Name Problem

What moved this year

Most commentary still runs on 2024 figures.

22 Apr 2026 — The silicon moved two generations. Stated

Ironwood is the seventh-generation TPU.

At Cloud Next, Google split the eighth in two.

TPU 8t for training, scaling to 9,600-chip superpods.

TPU 8i for latency-sensitive inference.

18 May 2026 — The financing moved off balance sheet. Stated

Blackstone and Google announced a US TPU cloud joint venture.

$5B initial equity. 500MW targeted for 2027. Former Google infrastructure executive Benjamin Treynor Sloss as CEO.

Blackstone holds the majority. Reported

Google keeps the silicon relationship without carrying the capital.

Apr 2026 — Google said it will sell the chips.

Google said it will deliver TPUs to a select group of customers in their own data centres, not only via Google Cloud. Stated / Q1 2026 call

That dilutes multi-tenant margins.

Read it as a hedge, not a mistake.

Selling accelerators up front pulls revenue forward against a capital base depreciating on a fixed clock.

The landlord is becoming an arms dealer.

5 Aug 2026 — The research layer restructured. Stated

Demis Hassabis became Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet.

Koray Kavukcuoglu took day-to-day control as SVP.

Jeff Dean left after 27 years to found Discovery Loop with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals and Quoc Le.

Alphabet is a founding investor and its cloud provider.

Alphabet shares fell roughly 4–5% on the day. Reported

Read the structure, not the departure.

When the research layer spins out, Alphabet funds it and rents it capacity.

The pattern doesn't stop at two names.

It reproduces.

08 · The Two-Name Problem

Four reasons this reading is wrong

If an argument only works when you skip the objections, it isn't an argument.

01 Anchor tenants build infrastructure.

Nobody finances a build this size against a long tail of small customers.

Ports, utilities and data centres all began with concentrated offtake.

Concentration may simply be what a young infrastructure business looks like.

02 The conversion clock is fast.

Over half the backlog converts within 24 months.

That undercuts my own "contracted, not collected" framing.

A delivery problem, not a credibility problem.

03 The numbers are estimates.

The 27% and 48% figures are UBS models, not filings.

If they're materially off, the concentration argument weakens with them.

04 Cloud is not the whole company.

Google Cloud was 20.7% of Alphabet's Q2 revenue — $24.768B of $119.8B. Filed

Advertising still funds everything.

Concentration inside a segment is not concentration inside the company.

09 · The Two-Name Problem

If you depend on one

Pick any infrastructure supplier for a five-year horizon and you're taking a position on its revenue base.

Cloud or on-prem.

Ask whose revenue funds the roadmap you're relying on.

Ask what proportion.

Ask whether capacity promised for 2027 is already committed to someone larger.

Standard due diligence for any supplier you'll depend on for five years.

We just skip it for hyperscalers.

The numbers are large enough to feel like weather.
